## Why we started KC Home Water

Our goal is to bring **high-quality water to homes across the Kansas City metro**. Most KC water is moderately hard to hard and disinfected with chloramine (we cover that in [Is Kansas City water hard?](/blog/is-kansas-city-water-hard)). It's safe to drink, but it's hard on water heaters and appliances, and plenty of people don't love how it tastes.

The fix isn't complicated. A softener, a whole-home carbon filter, and reverse osmosis at the kitchen sink handle almost everything. What frustrated us was the price tag. Water treatment often comes with a long in-home sales pitch, a big brand name, and a lot of overhead baked into the quote.

We think good water should be **accessible**, not a luxury.

## How we keep the price down

We do two things differently:

1. **We work directly with manufacturers.** Our systems are built from industry-standard parts, like **Fleck control valves**, fiberglass-reinforced tanks, and high-capacity resin. We buy them without a national dealer network's markup in between. (Our post on [what a water softener is](/blog/what-is-a-water-softener) explains why that matters.)
2. **We partner with local, reputable plumbers.** Instead of carrying a fleet of trucks and a big payroll, we team up with independent plumbers who already have a reputation in the community.

That lets us price our installs below many of our competitors, and we show you a [price range up front](/pricing) before you ever talk to us.

That last part matters to us. An independent plumber doesn't have a corporate brand to hide behind. Their name, their reviews, and their word-of-mouth referrals go with them into every home, so they carry their reputation through the whole install. That's exactly the kind of person we want working in your house.

## What to expect, start to finish

### 1. A short call

Start with a [quick estimate](/pricing), then book a call. We'll ask about your water source, your home, how many people live there, and where you want drinking water. No two-hour sales visit.

### 2. A written quote

After the call, you'll get a **written quote** with the system, the installation, and the price. The price in your quote doesn't change on install day.

### 3. Scheduling

We'll set a date that works for you and confirm which plumber is coming. Most whole-home installs take part of a day. Expect your water to be off for a few hours while the plumber works.

### 4. Install day

Your plumber will walk the space with you, confirm where everything goes, and get to work. A typical whole-home install includes:

- Shutting off the main and cutting into the line, usually near the water heater
- Installing a **bypass valve**, so the system can be serviced without shutting off your water
- Setting the **softener and brine tank**, plus the **carbon filter** if you're getting one
- Running the **drain line**, with the air gap plumbing codes call for
- For reverse osmosis: mounting the system under the sink, adding its dedicated faucet, and connecting the fridge or ice maker line if you want one

### 5. Start-up and walkthrough

Before leaving, your plumber brings the system online, sets it up for your water and household, and shows you how it all works: where the bypass is, how to add salt, and when filters need changing.

### 6. Follow-up

We'll check in after your install to make sure everything is working and answer any questions. You'll also get a filter replacement schedule, and we're happy to handle filter changes for you.

## Our install-day checklist

These are the things we expect to be done right on every job:

- [ ] **Confirm the plan** with the homeowner before any work starts
- [ ] **Protect the work area** and clear a path to it
- [ ] **Install a bypass valve** for easy service
- [ ] **Run the drain line correctly**, with a proper air gap
- [ ] **Program the control valve** for your household size, with a regeneration time when you're asleep
- [ ] **Run a first regeneration** and check that the brine tank fills and draws correctly
- [ ] **Check every connection for leaks** under full pressure
- [ ] **Flush the carbon filter and RO system** before you drink from them
- [ ] **Check water pressure** at the fixtures after the install
- [ ] **Clean up** and haul away packaging
- [ ] **Walk the homeowner through** the system, salt, bypass, and filter schedule
- [ ] **Leave the paperwork**: settings, filter schedule, and how to reach us

If something on that list isn't right, we want to hear about it, and we'll make it right.
