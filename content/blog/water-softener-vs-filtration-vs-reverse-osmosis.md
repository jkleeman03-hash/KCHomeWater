"Water treatment" covers a few very different systems, and it's easy to buy the wrong one. Here's what each does, in plain language.

## Water softener: fixes hardness

**What it does:** removes the calcium and magnesium that make water hard.

**How it works:** water flows through a tank of resin beads that grab hardness minerals and swap them for sodium. When the resin is full, the system rinses it with brine from a salt tank and starts fresh.

**Where it goes:** on your main water line, usually near the water heater, so every tap, shower, and appliance gets soft water.

**What you'll notice:** no more white crust on fixtures, fewer spots on dishes, better lather, and softer-feeling skin and laundry. It also stops new scale from building up inside your water heater and appliances.

**What it doesn't do:** a softener doesn't remove chlorine or chloramine, and it doesn't change how your water tastes.

## Whole-home filtration: fixes taste, smell, and chloramine

**What it does:** reduces the chlorine and chloramine utilities add for disinfection, along with many taste and odor issues. A sediment stage catches grit before it gets into your plumbing.

**How it works:** water passes through activated carbon, which grabs those chemicals. Chloramine lasts longer than chlorine, so it takes the right kind of carbon to reduce it.

**Where it goes:** also on your main water line, so water is filtered before it reaches any faucet or shower.

**What you'll notice:** better-tasting, better-smelling water everywhere, plus showers with less chemical smell.

## Reverse osmosis: the purest drinking water

**What it does:** pushes water through an extremely fine membrane that reduces dissolved solids and many contaminants, giving you bottled-water quality from its own faucet.

**Where it goes:** under the kitchen sink, where you drink and cook, often with a line to the fridge or ice maker. Tankless systems make water on demand instead of storing it, and a remineralization stage adds calcium and magnesium back so it tastes crisp instead of flat.

**Why not the whole house?** RO is slow and very thorough. That's exactly what you want for drinking water, but it's overkill for showers and laundry.

| Fixes hardness and scale | Yes | No | At one tap |

| Treats showers and laundry | Yes | Yes | No |

## What most Kansas City homes need

KC water is typically moderately hard to hard and disinfected with chloramine, so many homes do best with **a softener plus whole-home filtration**, which together handle hardness and taste in one install. Add **reverse osmosis** at the kitchen sink if you want the best drinking water or you're tired of buying bottled water.
