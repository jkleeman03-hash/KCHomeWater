Hard water is safe to drink, but it isn't kind to anything that heats or moves water. The calcium and magnesium in it build up as **scale**: the chalky white crust you see on faucets also forms inside your water heater, dishwasher, and washing machine, where you can't see it.

Here's what the research actually shows about how much that costs.

## Why heat makes it worse

Scale is mostly calcium carbonate, and heat speeds up how fast it comes out of the water. That's why the **water heater** takes the worst of it: every gallon of hot water you use leaves a little more mineral behind in the tank, on the heating element, and in the pipes leading out of it.

## The best data: the Battelle study

The most detailed testing comes from a 2009 study by the **Battelle Memorial Institute**. Battelle ran 30 water heaters (gas tank, electric tank, and tankless gas) side by side, half on softened water and half on hard water at **26 grains per gallon**, in a lab set up to speed up scaling. It also tested showerheads, faucets, dishwashers, and washing machines.

One thing to know up front: the study was funded by the Water Quality Research Foundation, the research arm of the water treatment industry. The testing was done by Battelle, an independent research lab, and the results below are taken straight from the study's executive summary.

### Water heaters

| Water heater | On softened water | On hard water (26 gpg) |
|---|---|---|
| Gas tank | Held 70.4% efficiency the whole test | Dropped from 70.4% to 67.4% within 2 years' equivalent use, and built up about **1.16 lb of scale per year** |
| Electric tank | About 0.03 lb of scale per year | About **2 lb of scale per year** on and around the heating element |
| Tankless gas | Held 80% efficiency | Dropped to 72% and **needed deliming after 1.6 years'** equivalent use. It only recovered to about 77% after cleaning. |

A few things stand out:

- **Every hard-water heater failed the test.** None of the units on hard water made it to the end, because scale clogged the outlet piping until it couldn't maintain enough flow. Every unit on softened water finished without problems.
- **Electric heaters lose life, not efficiency.** Their efficiency held steady because the element sits underwater, but Battelle notes that scale raises the element's operating temperature, which is expected to shorten its life.
- **Tankless heaters need regular deliming.** On hard water, that's a recurring maintenance cost that softened water avoids.

Battelle also used its results to estimate how much efficiency a gas tank water heater loses over its life at different hardness levels, and how often a tankless heater needs deliming:

| Water hardness | Gas tank heater: lifetime efficiency loss | Tankless heater: deliming needed about every |
|---|---|---|
| 5 gpg | 4.3% | 8.4 years |
| 10 gpg | 8.5% | 4.1 years |
| 15 gpg | 12.8% | 2.7 years |
| 20 gpg | 17.0% | 2.0 years |

Lost efficiency means more gas to heat the same water, every day, for the life of the heater.

### Showerheads and faucets

Low-flow showerheads on hard water **clogged after an average of 3,203 gallons**, and low-flow faucets clogged after the equivalent of **about 19 days** of use. Scale breaks loose from upstream plumbing and gets trapped in the screens. The ones on softened water ran the whole test without a problem.

### Dishwashers and washing machines

Battelle ran dishwashers and washing machines through 240 cycles (30 days) each. The units on hard water had **noticeable scale on every interior surface** and needed to be delimed and cleaned. The units on softened water were almost completely free of scale and looked like they'd clean up like new with a quick wipe.

## More detergent, hotter water

Follow-up studies funded by the same foundation in 2011 looked at cleaning results:

- **Dishwashers:** with softened water, households could use **up to 70% less dishwasher detergent** and still get the same results.
- **Laundry:** across nine detergents and nine common stains, softened water let testers **cut detergent by 50%** and wash in **60°F cold water** instead of 100°F hot water, with the same or better stain removal.

On hard water, you make up for it with more detergent and hotter water, and you pay for both.

## So how much shorter do appliances last?

Honestly, nobody has a reliable number. You'll see claims online that softened water makes appliances last 30% to 50% longer, but we couldn't trace those figures to independent research, so we don't repeat them.

What the testing does show is how the damage happens: scale clogs water heater outlets, forces regular deliming, shortens electric heating elements, coats the inside of dishwashers and washers, and plugs showerheads and faucets. Each of those is a repair, a service call, or an early replacement waiting to happen.

## What this means in Kansas City

Water around the Kansas City metro is typically **moderately hard to hard**. That's softer than the 26 gpg water in the Battelle tests, so the damage builds more slowly, but it builds the same way. To see your own number, look up your utility's annual water quality report and find **hardness**. Divide mg/L by 17.1 to get grains per gallon, then compare it to the tables above.

## How to protect your appliances

- **Soften the water.** A whole-home water softener removes the calcium and magnesium before they reach any appliance. That's the only fix that stops new scale everywhere at once.
- **Flush your tank water heater.** Draining the sediment every one to three years helps, and hard water calls for the more frequent end of that range.
- **Clean aerators and showerheads.** Soaking them in vinegar dissolves light scale.
- **Delime tankless heaters on schedule.** On hard water, check your manufacturer's recommended interval.
