Most Kansas City homes get their water from a city utility. But thousands of homes across the metro, especially in the rural edges, pump their own water from a **private well**. If that's you, nobody is testing or treating your water but you.

## How many KC-area homes are on well water?

The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency estimates there are about **10,500 private household wells** across the 14 counties of the Kansas City metro. Most homes have one well, so that's roughly 10,500 homes. The biggest numbers are in the outer counties:

## Nobody tests a private well but you

City water is tested on a set schedule by certified operators and has to meet federal drinking water standards. **Private wells don't.** In both Missouri and Kansas, testing and treating a household well is up to the owner. There's no required test, and no one will tell you if something changes.

That matters, because well water can change. Heavy rain, flooding, a nearby septic system, or fertilizer on surrounding fields can all affect what's coming out of your tap.

## What can be found in well water

As groundwater moves through soil and rock, it picks up whatever is there. Some of it is just a nuisance. Some of it can make you sick.

**Things that can affect your health:**

- **Bacteria.** Coliform bacteria and *E. coli* can get into a well from septic systems, animal waste, or surface water running in after heavy rain or flooding. In a Missouri Department of Health survey of 861 private wells, done with the CDC, **56% tested positive for coliform bacteria and 22% for _E. coli_**. That survey is from 1994, but bacteria remain the top concern for Missouri well owners.
- **Nitrate.** Nitrate comes mostly from fertilizer, manure, and septic systems. The Kansas Geological Survey calls it the most common inorganic contaminant in the state's groundwater. High levels are especially dangerous for babies under six months and can cause "blue baby syndrome." The federal limit for public water is 10 milligrams per liter.
- **Arsenic and other metals.** Some wells pick up naturally occurring arsenic and other metals from the rock around them. You can't see, smell, or taste them.

**Things that are hard on your home:**

- **Hardness.** Well water in this area is often hard, full of the same calcium and magnesium that leave scale on fixtures and inside water heaters.
- **Iron and manganese.** These leave orange, brown, or black stains in sinks, toilets, and laundry, and can give water a metallic taste.
- **Sulfur.** Hydrogen sulfide gas gives water a "rotten egg" smell.
- **Sediment.** Sand, silt, and grit can cloud water and wear out valves and appliances.

## Start with a test

You can't treat what you haven't measured. The EPA recommends testing a private well **every year for coliform bacteria and nitrate**, and again any time the water changes in taste, color, or smell, or after a flood. Before you rely on a new well, test for metals like arsenic too. Your county health department can tell you how to get a sample to a state-certified lab.

## What a well water treatment system does

There's no single "well filter." A well water treatment system is built in stages, and each stage handles a different problem. Your test results decide which stages you need:

- **Sediment filter.** Catches sand and grit first, so nothing downstream clogs.
- **Iron, manganese, and sulfur removal.** Turns these dissolved minerals into particles a filter can catch, which ends the staining and the rotten-egg smell.
- **Water softener.** Removes the calcium and magnesium that cause scale. Softeners can also handle small amounts of iron.
- **Carbon filter.** Improves taste and smell and reduces some chemicals.
- **UV disinfection.** Ultraviolet light kills bacteria, viruses, and parasites as water flows past, without adding any chemicals.
- **Reverse osmosis at the kitchen sink.** A softener and carbon filter don't remove nitrate or arsenic. A reverse osmosis system under the sink reduces both in your drinking and cooking water.

## Why it's worth doing

- **Your family's health.** Bacteria and nitrate don't announce themselves. Treatment, plus regular testing, means you're not guessing about the water your kids drink.
- **Your home.** Hard, iron-heavy water stains fixtures, clogs pipes, and wears out water heaters, dishwashers, and washing machines years early.
- **Water you actually want to drink.** No metallic taste, no rotten-egg smell, no orange stains in the tub.
