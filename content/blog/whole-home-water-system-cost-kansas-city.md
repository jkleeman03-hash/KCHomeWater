Most water treatment companies won't give you a number until a salesperson has spent two hours in your kitchen. We think you should know what to expect up front.

## The short answer

For homes across the Kansas City metro, our estimates run from about **$2,450 to $4,550**. Where you land depends on a few things about your home. You can [get your own estimate](/pricing) in under a minute.

## What moves the price

**City water or well water.** City water around KC is treated and fairly consistent, so we know what we're working with: hardness plus chloramine. Well water varies much more and can carry iron, sulfur, or sediment that needs extra treatment, so it costs more.

**Bathrooms.** Bathrooms tell us how much water your home can use at once. More showers and sinks running at the same time means the system has to move more water per minute without losing pressure, which takes a larger softener and filter tank.

**People in the home.** More people means more water through the system every day. A bigger household needs a softener with more capacity and goes through filter media faster.

**Drinking taps.** Reverse osmosis goes where you drink the water, usually the kitchen sink. Each additional tap where you want purified water adds another system or more line to reach it.

**Location.** Homes in Jackson and Johnson counties are in our core service area. Homes elsewhere in the metro may land a bit higher.

There's a fuller breakdown in [what matters and why](/pricing/explained).

## What to plan for after installation

- **Salt:** most homes add salt to the softener every month or two.
- **Filters:** carbon and RO filters need replacing periodically, usually every 6 to 12 months depending on the stage. You'll get a replacement schedule with your quote, and we can handle filter changes for you.

## How to get your exact price

An estimate is a range. Your exact price comes in a **written quote** after a short call, where we confirm a few details about your home. The price in your quote doesn't change on install day, and installation is done by a licensed, insured local plumber.
